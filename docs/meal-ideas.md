# Meal Ideas with AI

Figuring out "what's for dinner?" is one of the hardest parts of family life. This section shows you how to use free AI to plan meals, use what you already have, and keep grocery costs down.

## Step 1: List what you have

Open your fridge, freezer, and pantry. Write down a few ingredients you want to use before they go bad.

Example: chicken breasts, rice, frozen broccoli, a jar of salsa.

## Step 2: Ask for meal ideas

Type your ingredients into the AI chat. Be specific about constraints that matter to you.

Example prompt:

> "I have chicken breasts, rice, frozen broccoli, and salsa. Give me 3 dinner ideas for a family of 4, each ready in 30 minutes or less, with a grocery list of anything else I need."

## Step 3: Pick one and cook

Choose one idea, write it on your meal planner, and cook. If you get stuck mid-recipe, ask the AI:

> "I'm making chicken and rice. What can I do with leftover cooked chicken tomorrow?"

## Step 4: Save your favorites

Keep a list of meals your family actually enjoyed. Next week, ask the AI for ideas "similar to the chicken and rice my kids liked."

## Prompts for different situations

- Picky eaters: "Give me 5 dinner ideas a picky 4-year-old will eat, with no sauce touching the food."
- Tight budget: "Plan 5 dinners for under $40 total, using cheap staples like beans, rice, and pasta."
- Low energy: "Give me 3 dinners that need almost no chopping and one pan only."
- Leftovers: "I have leftover roast chicken and mashed potatoes. What can I make for lunch tomorrow?"
- Snacks: "Give me 5 after-school snack ideas that need no cooking."

## Meal planning routine (try this weekly)

1. Sunday evening: check the fridge and pantry.
2. Ask the AI for 4–5 dinner ideas using what you have.
3. Write the meals on the printable Weekly Meal Planner.
4. Make a grocery list for anything missing.
5. Shop, then check off each meal as you cook it.
